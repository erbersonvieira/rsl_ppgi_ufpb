# rsl_ppgi_ufpb
The absence of consistent haptic feedback remains a barrier to immersion, embodiment, and task performance in Immersive Virtual Environments (IVEs), particularly in skill transfer applications such as training and
simulation. Physical props used to substitute for virtual objects constitute a low-cost alternative, but the literature remains fragmented in terms of terminology, platforms, and evaluation methods. Following the PRISMA guidelines, this scoping review synthesized 117 primary studies on physical-virtual object substitution for providing haptic feedback in IVEs. The eight identified techniques were organized into an analytical spectrum of physical-virtual correspondence, ranging from strict one-to-one alignment to dynamic and reconfigurable forms of substitution. The review also mapped the platforms, tracking systems, technical and experimental limitations, and methods used to assess Presence and Sense of Agency (SoA). Presence was predominantly assessed using established and validated questionnaires, whereas SoA was rarely measured directly and was often inferred from related constructs. The studies indicate that using physical objects can provide tactile feedback and increase Presence compared with interfaces that rely exclusively on mid-air gestures. However, distortions that are tolerable for Presence may compromise SoA. The absence of standardized quantitative metrics for geometric similarity and the lack of adaptation of established SoA measurement paradigms and instruments to the context of haptic props constitute an identified gap in the literature. Based on the literature on bodily cognition, we propose adapting existing behavioral paradigms and psychometric instruments to the domain of haptic props, along with future research directions focused on safe, agency-preserving, and psychologically credible haptic interactions in immersive systems.
